# Physics accuracy review of the AIAA powered-descent paper

**Working review, 17 November 2026.** I reviewed the full manuscript *Convex Powered-Descent Guidance with Plant-Derived Constraints for a Jet-Vane Thrust-Vectored Electric Ducted Fan Testbed*, its extended abstract, the production models, the vehicle asset, the figure calculations, and the recorded mission summaries at revision `01bd4e188c84`. Equation, figure, and table numbers below refer to the full manuscript. [Reviewed full paper](https://github.com/Jacob19999/tvc-retro-propulsion/blob/01bd4e188c84a68d4347bc0a4a6843691a4f2204/Paper/aiaa_conference/tvc_convex_guidance_aiaa.pdf) · [Reviewed extended abstract](https://github.com/Jacob19999/tvc-retro-propulsion/blob/01bd4e188c84a68d4347bc0a4a6843691a4f2204/Paper/aiaa_conference/tvc_convex_guidance_abstract.pdf)

**Assessment.** The paper supports a simulation result about guiding its nominal EDF model. The hover force balance, gyrostat nutation frequency, first-order-hold translation equations, and principal planner counts reproduce. Several statements about the physical feasibility of the returned trajectories fail checks on the paper’s own cases. Those statements affect the abstract and conclusion. The hardware performance remains a prediction, especially where motor torque, wake area, and vane travel determine the available braking and attitude authority.

The first three checks used seed 20261116, the paper’s zero-based case IDs, 24 intervals, the default hard-terminal settings, and Clarabel 0.11.1. For the rate-constraint comparison I kept each original duration, both thrust bounds, the horizontal slew constraint, and the state and entry-envelope calculations. Only the inequality on the full vector change was removed. The measured maximum near 3.4% has a geometric explanation. The pointing constraint implies `||u|| ≥ u_z ≥ σ cos(15°)`, hence the relative gap cannot exceed **1 − cos(15°) = 3.4074%**. This explains the size of the ceiling without identifying a unique cause. [Planner implementation](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_guidance.py) · [Planner study](scripts/fig_planner_study.py)

For interval checks, write the linear thrust acceleration as `u(s) = u₀ + s Δu`, with `0 ≤ s ≤ 1`. Its smallest magnitude occurs at `s = clip(−u₀·Δu / ||Δu||², 0, 1)` for a nonconstant interval. The speed of the unit thrust direction is `||u × Δu|| / (Δt ||u||²)`. The numerator is constant along the interval, so the same minimum gives the exact peak angular speed. This calculation avoids time-grid sampling error. The guard applies half-spaces only to flagged nodes and can return the original solution if repair fails. Neither the guard nor a small node gap certifies every point of a trajectory. [First-order-hold and guard implementation](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_guidance.py)

## Propulsion, battery, and energy

**Motor loading and emergency braking (§IV.A, Table 1, Table 3).** The 4,649.6 rad/s reference comes from the motor’s *no-load* KV rating at 29.6 V. At that same modeled point the fan delivers 48 N gross thrust and takes 3,072 W of shaft power, or 0.661 N·m. A physical loaded motor requires voltage headroom above back EMF to pass torque-producing current. Treat this as an effective speed-to-thrust calibration until loaded RPM, bus voltage, current, and thrust are available together. Also identify whether the 120 A figure limits battery current, motor phase current, or both. The use of a constant torque cap and a separate bus-power cap is a modeling choice. [8S configuration](../../simulation/isaac/configs/hardware/planned_8s.yaml) · [Motor equations and parameter identification](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0020294019842607)

The **243 N/s emergency rate** is a command limit calculated from duty slew. With the paper’s own motor equation at hover, aerodynamic torque is 0.467 N·m. Even immediately applying the 0.76 N·m motor limit gives only **22.8 N/s upward net-thrust slew**, before voltage or energy limitations. At the endpoints of all first plans in this campaign, the positive scalar slew stayed below that torque-only ceiling, including the 45 fallback plans. The discrepancy concerns the emergency authority the formulation permits and the interpretation of “maximum braking.” Likewise, 0.76 N·m bounds motor torque. During braking the spool reaction can exceed it because rotor deceleration includes aerodynamic torque. The isolated torque-limited Fig. 2 trace reaches **0.98 N·m** on the reduction sequence. [Spool figure calculation](../physics_simulation/scripts/fig_spool.py) · [Controller rate calculations](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_adapter.py)

**Battery endurance and the energy objective (§§IV.A, V.A, V.F).** I reproduced 2.085 kW at level hover and the reported 243 s end time. The battery script allows a **2% speed deficit**, almost a 4% thrust deficit. The last logged sample within 0.01% of required hover speed was at **234.0 s**. At the final sample the rotor fraction was 0.8246 against 0.8409 required. Call 243 s the time to the stated speed-loss threshold. In Eq. 4, distinguish requested from deliverable power before taking the square root. The code clips requested power to the physically available maximum first. [Battery endurance calculation](../physics_simulation/scripts/fig_battery.py) · [Battery circuit](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/dynamics/battery_lipo.py)

Equation 21a prices quasi-steady electrical power at neutral-vane net thrust. Equation 3 also accounts for rotor kinetic-energy change and clips negative motor power because the pack receives no regeneration. The full model additionally has voltage sag, finite torque, and angle-dependent axial losses. The planner uses trapezoidal quadrature on the relaxation slack. Its Wh values therefore quantify a planning surrogate. Hover-speed rotor energy alone is **0.425 Wh**. For equal initial and final speeds the signed kinetic-energy change cancels, but clipping negative power can prevent cancellation in electrical energy. The reported +1.97% median guard cost is meaningful as a comparison of *planned* energy across newly chosen durations. ## Wake, vanes, and airframe

**The nozzle interpretation changes more than vane authority (Eqs. 6–12, §VIII).** At 48 N, a stationary uniform pressure-matched jet at 128 m/s implies an effective area of **0.00239 m²**, half the stated 0.00478 m² swept area. The 128 m/s value is consistent with an ideal contracted open-rotor far wake. Applying it at the vane station assumes that contraction or an equivalent velocity profile. The alternative 90.5 m/s follows only for a pressure-matched exit with the stated effective area. A real duct can have a pressure contribution, a nonuniform profile and a hub wake. The paper’s own alternate convention raises modeled hover mass flow 38% and full-speed net thrust 5.6%. Even with unchanged reference shaft power it changes predicted hover electrical power from **2.085 to 1.924 kW**. Its 3% change in full-speed vane authority cannot establish that planned energy, braking margin, or wind response are insensitive. [Jet-speed sensitivity](scripts/sens_jet_speed.py) · [NASA’s control-volume thrust equation](https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/thrust-force/)

**Vane authority is a small-angle derivative under a prescribed flow partition (§IV.C, Figs. 2–3).** The axial-flow derivation of Eq. 11 checks. Each modeled vane receives one quarter of the mass flux. The plotted `(T/4) sin α` adds an assumption that outflow turns by at most the vane deflection at the incident speed. Momentum conservation alone does not establish either condition for a real vane. The lift-slope dependence also remains finite through `1 − exp(−λ)`. “Weakly sensitive at the assumed loading” describes the demonstrated result. Full-model and axial-only forces at 15° are **2.667 and 2.691 N** respectively. The production ±0.035 rad finite difference returns a hover roll slope of 1.67245 N·m/rad. Reducing the probe to ±0.00001 rad yields 1.67382. Qualify the four-digit agreement and distinguish the 8.47 small-signal authority ratio from a force ratio at finite deflection. [Vane model](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/dynamics/coupled_jet.py) · [Force and authority calculations](../physics_simulation/scripts/fig_vane_force.py)

At fixed hover RPM and +15° common-mode deflection, the full model gives **+0.348 N·m on the vanes and +0.302 N·m net on the body** after the swirl reaction. At −15° the net is −0.307 N·m. Axial thrust simultaneously falls from **30.45 N to 28.15 N** at +15°. The replica retains neutral-vane thrust while applying linear vane torque and side force. That removes a direct coupling between attitude correction and altitude. Label the shaded authority in Fig. 2 by its torque definition, distinguish 15° mechanical from 14° commanded travel, and carry this omission into the interpretation of the mismatch campaign. [Mission plant used by the figures](../physics_simulation/scripts/plant.py) · [Closed-loop replica](scripts/fig_closed_loop.py)

**Geometry and ground effect (Table 1, §§III.A and VIII).** I independently read the USD mass properties and mesh. The body mass, center of mass with the FRD sign conversion, diagonal inertia, and four 1 g vanes agree with the asset. The body mesh including legs spans approximately **0.313 × 0.313 × 0.422 m**. The 0.12 m diameter and 0.35 m length describe the aerodynamic reference body. The CAD nozzle station is +0.055 m FRD, rounded to +0.050 m in the model. Since damping scales with the squared lever arm to the center of mass, 0.065 versus 0.060 m changes that term by about 17%. The vane meshes span approximately 0.0286 × 0.0786 m in planform, about 0.00225 m² per rectangular plate. Identify the 0.002 m² value as rounded or effective. A geometric centroid at 0.033 m gives one possible yaw lever arm. Establishing an upper bound on the error requires information about the pressure distribution. [USD geometry measurement](../../simulation/isaac/tools/measure_duct_stations.py) · [Vehicle parameters](../../simulation/isaac/configs/vehicle/edf_drone_v2.yaml)

The ground-effect statement has a geometric error. The upright body origin is 0.3125 m above the pad at touchdown and 0.8125 m at the gate. The modeled nozzle is thus **0.7625 m above the pad at the gate, about 8.5 fan diameters**, and about **2.9 diameters** at touchdown. The paper says the gate is within two diameters. Use nozzle-to-pad clearance for jet impingement and distinguish it from leg clearance. Geometry alone does not quantify a ground-effect force. [Gate construction](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_adapter.py) · [Touchdown height](scripts/fig_closed_loop.py)

**Intake forces and passive damping (§§IV.D–F, VII.E).** The signs and dimensions of the intake and duct Coriolis terms check for the stated straight, centered duct with quasi-steady throughflow. Taking the moment about the center of mass gives `−ṁ zₑ² ω⊥` for their pure-rotation sum. Evaluating each force at its own point gives the paper’s nonpositive combined power. The predicted 1.26 N crosswind intake force and 0.41 N body drag at 4 m/s also reproduce. These checks verify internal bookkeeping. The mass flow, freestream capture, effective lip force location and omitted duct pressure loads still control the physical force and moment. A planner drag term stays affine if mass flow is prescribed. The plant’s thrust-dependent mass flow multiplied by velocity generally introduces a nonlinear product. [Drag calculation](../physics_simulation/scripts/fig_drag.py) · [Intake-model revision and wind records](../../simulation/isaac/docs/physics_review_2026-09-29.md)

The statement that the late damping changes are “three orders of magnitude below the vane authority” compares **N·m·s/rad** with **N·m/rad**. Compare torques at a specified body rate and vane angle, or compare modal decay. The modeled duct term alone, 0.00112 N·m·s/rad, exceeds the 0.000894 vane-flow roll damping. The small-amplitude scalar roll coefficient is about **0.00203 N·m·s/rad**. The 0.00207 total includes quadratic cylinder drag evaluated at 1 rad/s. An exact amplitude-independent exponential decay does not follow from that total. The recorded 5.5° crosswind lean also preceded the change from a 0.10 m intake moment arm to 0.007 m. It belongs to that earlier configuration. ## Rotation, sensing, and control

**The gyrostat calculation survives review with a precise energy definition (§IV.F, Fig. 5).** At hover, `H = 0.782 N·m·s` and `H/(2π Ixx) = 2.49 Hz` for the symmetric, zero-yaw-rate model. The explicit-Euler **transverse** energy amplification is `[1 + (ωₙ Δt)²]ⁿ`, giving 56.9 in two seconds at 120 Hz and zero axial body spin. In the counter-rotating example the transverse multiplier is about 6.22 × 10¹⁰. The plotted **body** kinetic-energy ratio, which includes the initial axial energy, is 6.83 × 10⁹. The figure excludes rotor spin energy and omits the engine’s angular-momentum projection. The midpoint/Cayley run independently reproduced unit body-energy ratio and world angular-momentum drift around 10⁻¹⁵ N·m·s. State the invariant and the isolated, torque-free, constant-rotor-speed conditions in both abstracts. The operating powered vehicle exchanges energy and angular momentum with its wake. [Gyro experiment and energy definition](../physics_simulation/scripts/fig_gyro.py) · [Production integration bridge](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/dynamics/rotor_reaction.py)

**Actuator feedback and phase (§§IV.C, VI.B, VII.C).** Eq. 13 implements an error dead zone. Its settled error can lie anywhere inside the band. Mechanical backlash additionally depends on which gear face is engaged after reversal. The estimate `s = j + τⱼ dj/dt` follows from the assumed first-order joint dynamics and ideal rate feedback. The simulation observation contains exact joint angles, rates and normalized rotor speed, even when the navigation channels have sensor noise. With noise disabled, the observation path returns the original state. Replace the blanket statement that the flight computer never reads simulator truth with a channel-by-channel description of what was sensed or supplied ideally. Actuator observability on the aircraft is an unresolved assumption of the regulator. The 0.050 and 0.025 s first-order lags contribute approximately **59.4°** of phase lag at 2.49 Hz. A half-sample delay at 30 Hz contributes about 14.9° more. A claim of over 90° requires the remaining dynamics, delay and loop transfer function to be identified. The calculated PD unstable poles and LQR decay rates reproduce on the specified linearization. Those poles support a local stability comparison. They do not predict the amplitude or prove the cause of a nonlinear limit cycle. Use the separate nonlinear runs for that evidence. State that the Fig. 10 calculation applies its oscillatory-mode damping statistic to modes above 0.5 Hz. In Table 4, rounded values of 3.70 versus 3.9°/s differ by 5.1%, so “about 5%” fits the precision. ## Guidance formulation and landing evidence

**State what the fixed-duration solve guarantees (§§V.A–G).** Equation 25 integrates the point-mass dynamics exactly for linear thrust and constant gravity. The signs, units, trapezoidal velocity weights, and one-third/one-sixth position weights check. The energy epigraph exponent 2/3 is correct. The implemented program uses second-order **and power cones**. A rational exponent admits an equivalent lifted SOCP formulation, but the code sends a native power cone to Clarabel. The fixed-duration subproblem is convex. The duration search is external, and the one-pass repair may require a second solve. In the abstract, replace “exact SOCP” and unqualified “global optimality” with the formulation actually solved. [Cone assembly](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_guidance.py) · [Clarabel cone definitions](https://clarabel.org/stable/api_cone_types/)

Several constraints have a wider operating range in the code than in Eq. 21. A routine replan can initialize position, velocity **and thrust** from the preceding reference. A measured thrust vector outside the permissible pointing cone is projected, an out-of-band initial magnitude can get ramped bounds, and an excessive entry sink rate gets a time-decaying relaxation. The constant-deceleration envelope assumes that upward acceleration is available immediately. At 26 V and 15° tilt the reserved ceiling provides only **0.098 m/s²** upward acceleration, below the **0.157 m/s²** used by the envelope at that voltage. Tilting back takes time. The envelope is a design margin, not a stopping-distance certificate for the six-degree-of-freedom vehicle. List its entry exceptions and the configured 0.35, 0.4, 0.5 and 0.6 allocations as design choices. [Entry and sink constraints](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_guidance.py) · [Replan initialization](../../simulation/isaac/tvc_env/controllers/convex_adapter.py)

**The duration survey supports a narrower and still informative finding (§VII.B).** I reproduced increasing sampled cost and the 0.577–0.579 Wh/s slope for the eligible curves in the relaxed duration study. The study turns the gap guard off for the 60-start sweep. Of these, 53 had at least one solved point on the 0.5 s grid. This supports hover-power dominance over the sampled durations. It does not establish continuous monotonicity, a single feasible interval for the guarded problem, or universal superiority of the fastest physical landing. A hard-terminal search failure also changes several conditions at once when switching to the fallback, including the thrust ceiling, slew, endpoint and path requirements. The near-zero gate errors reproduced for the 45 fallback solutions cannot isolate the cause of the original search failures. ## Evidence and revision priorities

I reproduced the original CPU-suite count of **437 passed, two skipped**, the nominal 400-run replica campaign, the 400-start planner study with and without the guard, the duration survey, and the spool, gyro, vane, drag, attitude, battery and representative-plan calculations. I checked the five 100-run variant summaries against their saved per-run records. I also read the USD independently and ran its mass-property validator. The engine results were reviewed against repository records. I did not re-fly Isaac Sim missions or test any hardware.

The first revision should correct the four feasibility claims in the opening table and the low-voltage exception. The central numerical story then remains clear: the nominal model closes the landing loop across the tested starts, monitored node gaps are small after repair, and energy grows approximately at hover power across the sampled durations. Apply the same qualifications to the extended abstract, which repeats the stronger claims.

For hardware identification, the measurements that would resolve the largest physical uncertainties are loaded RPM, thrust, current and bus voltage measured together, followed by net axial force and roll/yaw moments during a vane sweep. Identify rotor inertia independently when fitting coast-down data. A coast-down trace alone measures the ratio of aerodynamic drag torque to inertia, and a nutation frequency measures the ratio of rotor momentum to transverse inertia. Those ratios provide valuable cross-checks once either constituent has an independent measurement.
